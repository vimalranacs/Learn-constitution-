### 3. Compelling Opening Question / Real-World Problem
> **Can a Citizen File a Lawsuit Against the Central Government Directly in the Supreme Court Under Article 131?**  
> Suppose a private telecom company or an individual citizen has a contract dispute with the Union Ministry of Communications and wants to skip the District Court and High Court by filing an original suit directly in the Supreme Court under **`[📜 Article 131](node:art_131)`** (*"Original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court"*).  
> Conversely, suppose the **State of Kerala** challenges the constitutional validity of a central statute enacted by Parliament and files an original suit against the **Government of India** under **`[📜 Article 131](node:art_131)`**, while the Union Attorney-General argues that a State cannot use Article 131 to challenge a central statute!  
> **The Question:** Why can a private citizen or corporation **NEVER** be a party to a suit under `[📜 Article 131](node:art_131)` (whereas they *can* file a Fundamental Rights writ petition under `[📜 Article 32](node:art_32)`)? And how does `Article 131` resolve disputes between the Union and the States—and which specific federal dispute is explicitly **carved out** of Article 131 by `[📜 Article 262](node:art_262)`?

### 4. Beginner-Friendly Foundation (Before Advanced Terminology)
`[PEDAGOGICAL_ANALOGY]`  
Dr. B.R. Ambedkar explained in the Constituent Assembly (CAD Vol. VII, p. 37) that while India is a federation dividing legislative and executive powers between the Union and the States, **India has a single integrated system of courts** administering both Union laws and State laws:
* **Pyramid Base — `[🏛️ District & Subordinate Courts](node:inst_subordinate_courts)` (`Articles 233–237`, PDF pp. 140–141):** In every district, the **District and Sessions Judge** heads the civil and criminal trial judiciary (Civil Judges on the civil side; Chief Judicial Magistrate and Judicial Magistrates on the criminal side). This is where $90\%$ of citizens first encounter the justice system.
* **Middle Tier — `[🏛️ High Courts in the States](node:inst_high_courts)` (`Articles 214–231`, PDF pp. 133–139):** Courts of record (`Art. 215`) hearing first/second appeals from District Courts, exercising broad original **Writ Jurisdiction under `[📜 Article 226](node:art_226)`**, and exercising judicial and administrative **superintendence over all courts and tribunals** throughout their territories under **`[📜 Article 227](node:art_227)`** (held part of the Basic Structure in *L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India*, `(1997) 3 SCC 261`).
* **Apex Tier — `[🏛️ The Supreme Court of India](node:inst_supreme_court)` (`Articles 124–147`, PDF pp. 88–99):** The final court of appeal for the entire Republic, the exclusive arbiter of federal disputes (`Art. 131`), the guarantor of Fundamental Rights (`Art. 32`), and the court whose declared law is **binding on all courts within the territory of India (`[📜 Article 141](node:art_141)`)**.

| **1. Court of Record & Contempt** | **`[📜 Article 129](node:art_129)`** (p. 92) | The Supreme Court is a **court of record** (its records have evidentiary value and cannot be questioned) and has all the powers of such a court including the power to punish for **contempt of itself** (mirrored for High Courts in **`[📜 Article 215](node:art_215)`**). |
| **2. Exclusive Original Federal Jurisdiction** | **`[📜 Article 131](node:art_131)`** (p. 92) | Exclusive to the Supreme Court in any dispute involving a question of law or fact on which the existence or extent of a legal right depends **ONLY between:** (a) the Government of India and one or more States, (b) the Government of India and any State(s) on one side and one or more other States on the other, or (c) two or more States. *(Private citizens/corporations cannot be parties! Excludes pre-Constitution treaties `Art. 131 Proviso` and Inter-State River Water disputes excluded by Parliament under `[📜 Article 262](node:art_262)`).* |
| **3. Original Writ Jurisdiction** | **`[📜 Article 32](node:art_32)`** (p. 45) | Original (though **not exclusive**, since High Courts share concurrent writ power under `Art. 226`) jurisdiction to issue the 5 writs (*Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, Certiorari*) **for the enforcement of Part III Fundamental Rights**. |
| **4. Appellate Jurisdiction (Constitutional, Civil, Criminal)** | **`[📜 Articles 132, 133, 134, 134A](node:art_132)`** (pp. 93–94) | • **`Art. 132` (Constitutional):** Appeal from any High Court judgment/decree/final order (civil, criminal, or other) if the High Court certifies under `Art. 134A` that the case involves a **substantial question of law as to the interpretation of this Constitution**.<br>• **`Art. 133` (Civil):** Appeal if HC certifies a substantial question of law of general importance that in the opinion of the HC needs to be decided by the SC.<br>• **`Art. 134` (Criminal):** Appeal as of right if the HC on appeal reversed an order of acquittal of an accused person and sentenced him to death (`134(1)(a)`), or withdrew a case for trial before itself and sentenced the accused to death (`134(1)(b)`), or certifies under `134(1)(c)`. |
| **5. Special Leave to Appeal (Plenary Residual Power)** | **`[📜 Article 136](node:art_136)`** (p. 94) | Discretionary plenary power to grant **Special Leave to Appeal (SLP)** from any judgment, decree, determination, sentence, or order in any cause or matter passed or made by **any court or tribunal in the territory of India** (except Armed Forces courts/tribunals under `Art. 136(2)`). |
| **6. Review & Curative Jurisdiction** | **`[📜 Article 137](node:art_137)`** (p. 95) | Power to review any judgment pronounced or order made by it. In *Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra* (`(2002) 4 SCC 388`), a 5-Judge Bench devised the **Curative Petition** to prevent gross miscarriage of justice even after dismissal of a Review Petition. |
| **7. Binding Precedent & Complete Justice** | **`[📜 Article 141](node:art_141)` & `[📜 Article 142](node:art_142)`** (p. 96) | • **`Art. 141`:** The law declared by the Supreme Court shall be **binding on all courts** within the territory of India (*stare decisis*; Note: the SC itself is not bound by its own earlier decisions and can overrule them via a larger bench, *Bengal Immunity Co. v. State of Bihar*, 1955).<br>• **`Art. 142(1)`:** Power to pass such decree or make such order as is necessary for doing **"complete justice"** in any cause or matter pending before it (cannot be used to override express substantive statutory provisions against public policy, *Supreme Court Bar Association v. | **8. Presidential Reference (Advisory Jurisdiction)** | **`[📜 Article 143](node:art_143)`** (p. 96) | If at any time it appears to the President that a question of law or fact has arisen of such public importance that it is expedient to obtain the opinion of the Supreme Court, the President may refer it to the Court (heard by a **Constitution Bench of at least 5 Judges** under **`[📜 Article 145(3)](node:art_145)`**). Under `143(1)` the Court *may* report its opinion (and may decline for good reasons, as in the 1993 *Ismail Faruqui* reference), whereas under `143(2)` (pre-Constitution treaties) the Court *shall* report its opinion. |

#### Section 5.2: Judicial Independence, Appointments (`Collegium` vs. `NJAC`), and Removal (`Article 124(4)`)
`[CONST_TEXT]` `[JUDICIAL_HOLDING]`  
How does the Constitution insulate Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts from executive or legislative pressure?
1. **Security of Tenure & Strict Removal Procedure (`[📜 Article 124(4)–(5)](node:art_124)` & `[📜 Article 217(1)(b)](node:art_217)`, Regulated by the Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968):**
   * A Judge of the Supreme Court (retires at age **65**) or a High Court (retires at age **62**, raised from 60 by the 15th Amendment, 1963) **cannot** be removed by the President at pleasure.
   * Removal is permissible **ONLY** on the ground of **proved misbehaviour or incapacity**, after an address by **each House of Parliament** supported by a **majority of the total membership of that House AND a majority of not less than two-thirds of the members of that House present and voting** has been presented to the President in the **same session** (and only after a 3-member inquiry committee consisting of a sitting SC Judge, a Chief Justice of a High Court, and a distinguished jurist under the **Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968** has investigated and found the charges proved!). *To date, no Supreme Court or High Court Judge has ever been removed by completion of this process.*
2. **The Four Judges Cases & The Collegium System (`Article 124(2)`):**
   * `Article 124(2)` states that every Judge of the Supreme Court shall be appointed by the President after **"consultation"** with such of the Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts as the President may deem necessary (with mandatory consultation with the Chief Justice of India for other Judges).
   * *First Judges Case (S.P. Gupta, 1981):* Held that "consultation" did not mean "concurrence", giving the Union Executive primacy.
   * *Second Judges Case (1993, 9-Judge Bench) & Third Judges Case (1998, 9-Judge Bench):* Overruled *S.P. Gupta*; held that to preserve **Judicial Independence (part of the Basic Structure)**, "consultation" with the CJI means **"concurrence"** formed collectively by a **Collegium** consisting of the **Chief Justice of India + the 4 senior-most Judges of the Supreme Court** (for SC appointments; and CJI + 2 senior-most SC Judges for High Court appointments).
   * *Fourth Judges Case (`[⚖️ NJAC Case, 2015](node:judg_njac)`, `(2016) 5 SCC 1`):* Struck down the **99th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2014** (`Articles 124A–124C`) and the NJAC Act by a 4:1 majority because giving the Union Law Minister and two non-judicial members a veto over judicial appointments compromised judicial independence under `[📜 Article 50](node:art_50)` and the Basic Structure.

### 6. Historical, Philosophical & Institutional Context
`[HIST_EVIDENCE]` `[CONST_TEXT]`  
Notice **`[📜 Article 145(3)](node:art_145)`** (PDF p. 97): *"The minimum number of Judges who are to sit for the purpose of deciding any case involving a substantial question of law as to the interpretation of this Constitution or for the purpose of hearing any reference under article 143 shall be **five**."* That is why every major constitutional case in Indian history is decided by a **Constitution Bench of 5, 7, 9, 11, or 13 Judges**!

### 7. Everyday Indian Civic Examples & Analogies
`[CONST_TEXT]` `[JUDICIAL_HOLDING]`  
* **What Happened When Tribunals Were Created to Replace High Courts? (`Articles 323A–323B` & *L. Chandra Kumar*, 1997):** The 42nd Amendment (1976) inserted `[📜 Part XIVA (Articles 323A & 323B)](node:part_XIVA)` (PDF pp. 213–215) allowing Administrative Tribunals (like CAT) to exclude the jurisdiction of High Courts so litigants could only appeal straight to the Supreme Court under Article 136. In *L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India* (`(1997) 3 SCC 261`, 7-Judge Bench), the Supreme Court **struck down the clauses excluding High Court jurisdiction** (`Art. 323A(2)(d)` and `Art. 323B(3)(d)`), holding that **judicial review by High Courts under `[📜 Articles 226 and 227](node:art_226)` is an unamendable part of the Basic Structure**, and consequently every order of an Administrative Tribunal is subject to scrutiny before a **Division Bench of the territorial High Court**!

### 8. Verbatim Constitutional Articles & Primary Sources
`[CONST_TEXT]`  
* **Article 131, Part V, Chapter IV (Official 2026 Constitution PDF, p. 92):**
  > **131. Original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.—**Subject to the provisions of this Constitution, the Supreme Court shall, to the exclusion of any other court, have original jurisdiction in any dispute—  
  > (a) between the Government of India and one or more States; or  
  > (b) between the Government of India and any State or States on one side and one or more other States on the other; or  
  > (c) between two or more States,  
  > if and in so far as the dispute involves any question (whether of law or fact) on which the existence or extent of a legal right depends:  
  > Provided that the said jurisdiction shall not extend to a dispute arising out of any treaty, agreement, covenant, engagement, *sanad* or other similar instrument which, having been entered into or executed before the commencement of this Constitution, continues in operation after such commencement, or which provides that the said jurisdiction shall not extend to such a dispute.
* **Article 141 & Article 142(1), Part V, Chapter IV (Official 2026 Constitution PDF, p. 96):**
  > **141. Law declared by Supreme Court to be binding on all courts.—**The law declared by the Supreme Court shall be binding on all courts within the territory of India.  
  > **142. Enforcement of decrees and orders of Supreme Court and orders as to discovery, etc.—**(1) The Supreme Court in the exercise of its jurisdiction may pass such decree or make such order as is necessary for doing complete justice in any cause or matter pending before it, and any decree so passed or order so made shall be enforceable throughout the territory of India in such manner as may be prescribed by or under any law made by Parliament and, until provision in that behalf is so made, in such manner as the President may by order prescribe.

### 10. Common Misconceptions & Their Corrections
* **Misconception:** *"When the Supreme Court resolves a water-sharing dispute between Karnataka and Tamil Nadu over the Cauvery River, it exercises its Original Federal Jurisdiction under Article 131."*
  * **Correction (`CONST_TEXT`):** Look at the opening words of `[📜 Article 131](node:art_131)`: ***"Subject to the provisions of this Constitution..."*** And now turn to **`[📜 Article 262(1)–(2)](node:art_262)`** (PDF p. 181): Parliament may by law provide for the adjudication of any dispute with respect to the use, distribution, or control of the waters of any **inter-State river or river valley**, AND **may by law provide that neither the Supreme Court nor any other court shall exercise jurisdiction in respect of any such dispute**! Parliament enacted the **Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956** (Section 11) barring Article 131 jurisdiction over water disputes and referring them to dedicated Water Disputes Tribunals (though once a Tribunal gives its final award, the Supreme Court can hear an appeal against the Tribunal's award via its plenary **Special Leave power under `[📜 Article 136](node:art_136)`**, *State of Karnataka v. State of Tamil Nadu*, `(2017) 3 SCC 362`!).

### 4. Beginner-Friendly Foundation (Before Advanced Terminology)
`[PEDAGOGICAL_ANALOGY]`  
When India adopted **Universal Adult Suffrage (`[📜 Article 326](node:art_326)`)** in 1949—granting every adult man and woman the right to vote regardless of literacy, wealth, caste, or gender (with the voting age lowered from 21 to **18 years** by the **61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988**, effective 1989)—western commentators called it *"the biggest gamble in history."*

To make sure that gamble succeeded, the framers built a **single, permanent, autonomous constitutional umpire (`[🏛️ Election Commission of India](node:inst_eci)`, `[📜 Article 324](node:art_324)`)**:
* In many countries, elections are run by the Home Ministry or by partisan state governors. In India, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar placed election superintendence in an independent Chapter (`Part XV`) so the ruling party can never manipulate voter rolls or election dates.
* Furthermore, `[📜 Article 325](node:art_325)` (PDF p. 217) abolishes colonial communal electorates by declaring: *"There shall be **one general electoral roll** for every territorial constituency for election to either House of Parliament or to the House or either House of the Legislature of a State and no person shall be ineligible for inclusion in any such roll or claim to be included in any special electoral roll for any such constituency on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex or any of them."*

### 5. Detailed Teachable Sections

#### Section 5.1: Constitutional vs. Statutory Electoral Framework (`Part XV` vs. `RPA 1950` vs. `RPA 1951`)
`[CONST_TEXT]` `[STATUTORY_LAW]`  
Students and practitioners must never confuse the three layers of Indian election law:

| Legal Instrument | Authority Type | What It Specifically Governs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. `[📜 Part XV of the Constitution (Articles 324–329)](node:part_XV)`** *(PDF pp. 216–219)* | `CONST_TEXT` | • **`Art. 324`:** Superintendence, direction, and control of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President, and Vice-President vested in the ECI; CEC removable only like a Supreme Court Judge (`Proviso to Art. 324(5)`).<br>• **`Art. 325`:** One general electoral roll; no exclusion on religion, race, caste, or sex.<br>• **`Art. 326`:** Elections to Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies on the basis of **adult suffrage (18+ years)**.<br>• **`Art. 327` & `328`:** Power of Parliament (and subsidiarily State Legislatures) to make laws with respect to elections and delimitation.<br>• **`Art. 329`:** Bar to interference by courts in delimitation laws (`329(a)`) and mandate that no election to Parliament or a State Legislature shall be called in question except by an **election petition** presented to such authority and in such manner as provided by law (`329(b)`). |
| **2. `[⚙️ Representation of the People Act, 1950 (Act 43 of 1950)](node:proc_rpa_1951)`** | `STATUTORY_LAW` | • Allocation of seats in the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies/Councils.<br>• Delimitation of constituencies.<br>• **Preparation and revision of electoral rolls**, qualifications of voters ("ordinarily resident"), Chief Electoral Officers (CEOs), and Electoral Registration Officers (EROs). |
| **3. `[⚙️ Representation of the People Act, 1951 (Act 43 of 1951)](node:proc_rpa_1951)`** | `STATUTORY_LAW` | • **Actual conduct of elections** (notifications, Returning Officers, polling, counting, declaration of results).<br>• **Statutory qualifications and disqualifications** for membership of Parliament and State Legislatures (**Sections 8 to 11A**: conviction for specified offences or sentence of imprisonment for $\ge 2$ years under `Section 8(3)`—*Lily Thomas v. Union of India*, `(2013) 7 SCC 653` struck down `Section 8(4)` so sitting MPs/MLAs are disqualified *immediately* upon conviction/sentence!).<br>• **Registration of political parties** (`Section 29A`).<br>• **Corrupt practices** (`Section 123`) and **Election Petitions** tried in the first instance by the **High Court** (`Section 80A`, inserted in 1966) with appeal to the Supreme Court (`Section 116A`). |

#### Section 5.2: The Tenth Schedule (Anti-Defection Law, 52nd & 91st Amendments) & *Kihoto Hollohon*
`[CONST_TEXT]` `[JUDICIAL_HOLDING]`  
Inserted by the **52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985** (`[📜 Articles 102(2) & 191(2)](node:art_102)`, `[📜 Tenth Schedule](node:sched_10)`, PDF pp. 376–379) to combat the *"Aaya Ram, Gaya Ram"* menace of political defections:
* **Four Grounds of Disqualification (`Paragraph 2` of the Tenth Schedule):**
  1. **Voluntarily Giving Up Party Membership (`Para 2(1)(a)`):** An elected member of a political party is disqualified if they "voluntarily give up" their membership of such political party. *(In Ravi S. Naik v. Union of India, `1994 Supp (2) SCC 641`, the Supreme Court held that "voluntarily giving up membership" is wider than formal resignation and can be inferred from a member's conduct, such as publicly campaigning for a rival party or approaching the Governor to topple their own party's government!)*
  2. **Voting or Abstaining Contrary to Party Whip (`Para 2(1)(b)`):** If the member votes or abstains from voting in the House contrary to any direction issued by their political party without obtaining prior permission, and such act has not been condoned by the party within **15 days**.
  3. **Independent Member Joining a Party (`Para 2(2)`):** An elected member who was elected as an **Independent** (not set up by any political party) is disqualified **immediately if they join ANY political party after the election**.
  4. **Nominated Member Joining a Party After 6 Months (`Para 2(3)`):** A **nominated** member of a House is permitted to join a political party **within 6 months** of taking their seat; if they join any political party **after the expiry of 6 months**, they are disqualified!
* **The Merger Exception (`Paragraph 4`, after the 91st Amendment Act, 2003 Deleted `Paragraph 3`):**
  * Originally, Paragraph 3 exempted a "split" by $1/3$rd of a legislature party. Because $1/3$rd splits enabled wholesale horse-trading, the **91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 completely omitted Paragraph 3**!
  * Today, under `Paragraph 4`, a member is protected from disqualification only if their original political party merges with another political party **AND not less than two-thirds ($2/3$rds) of the members of the legislature party** have agreed to such merger. *(In Subhash Desai v. Principal Secretary, Governor of Maharashtra, `2023 INSC 513`, a 5-Judge Constitution Bench clarified that the Speaker/ECI must look at the **original political party**, not merely a breakaway faction of the legislature party, and that a Speaker cannot recognize two rival whips without adjudicating who constitutes the real political party).*
* **Judicial Review under `[⚖️ Kihoto Hollohon v. Zachilhu (1992)](node:judg_kihoto)` (`1992 Supp (2) SCC 651`):**
  * The 5-Judge Bench **upheld** the Tenth Schedule as compatible with parliamentary democracy, **but struck down Paragraph 7** (which had barred the jurisdiction of all courts), holding that the Speaker/Chairman deciding a disqualification petition under Paragraph 6 acts as a **Statutory Tribunal** and their final order is subject to **judicial review by High Courts (`Arts. 226/227`) and the Supreme Court (`Art. 136`)** for *mala fides*, violation of natural justice, perversity, or constitutional infirmity.

#### Section 5.3: The 106th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2023 (`Articles 239AA, 330A, 332A, 334A`)
`[CONST_TEXT]`  
Incorporated in the Official 1 May 2026 Constitution PDF (pp. 145, 221–224), the **`[📜 Constitution (One Hundred and Sixth Amendment) Act, 2023](node:amend_106)`** (*Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam*):
1. Inserted **`[📜 Article 239AA(2)(ba)](node:art_239AA)`** reserving **one-third ($33\%$)** of the seats in the Legislative Assembly of the NCT of Delhi for women.
2. Inserted **`[📜 Article 330A](node:art_330A)`** reserving **one-third** of all seats (including one-third of the seats reserved for SCs and STs under `Art. 330`) for women in the **House of the People (`Lok Sabha`)**.
3. Inserted **`[📜 Article 332A](node:art_332A)`** reserving **one-third** of all seats (including one-third of SC/ST seats) for women in the **Legislative Assembly of every State**.
4. Inserted **`[📜 Article 334A](node:art_334A)`** providing that these reservations shall come into effect after an exercise of **delimitation** is undertaken after the relevant figures for the first census taken after the commencement of the 106th Amendment Act, 2023 have been published, and shall continue for a period of **15 years** (extendable by law made by Parliament).

### 6. Historical, Philosophical & Institutional Context
`[HIST_EVIDENCE]` `[JUDICIAL_HOLDING]`  
Examine the protection of the **Election Commission of India (`[📜 Article 324(5)](node:art_324)`, PDF p. 217)**:
* The **Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)** shall not be removed from his office except in like manner and on the like grounds as a **Judge of the Supreme Court** (`Art. 124(4)`), and the conditions of service of the CEC shall not be varied to his disadvantage after his appointment.
* The **other Election Commissioners** (and Regional Commissioners) shall not be removed from office except **on the recommendation of the Chief Election Commissioner** (*T.N. Seshan, Chief Election Commissioner of India v. Union of India*, `(1995) 4 SCC 611`, 5-Judge Bench holding that the CEC and Election Commissioners enjoy co-equal voting power on the multi-member Commission and make decisions by majority).

### 7. Everyday Indian Civic Examples & Analogies
`[JUDICIAL_HOLDING]` `[STATUTORY_LAW]`  
* **Your Right to Know a Candidate's Criminal Record & Assets (`Article 19(1)(a)` + Form 26 Affidavit) and NOTA:** Why must every candidate standing for Lok Sabha or Vidhan Sabha file a sworn affidavit (**Form 26** under Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961) disclosing their criminal antecedents, assets, liabilities, and educational qualifications? Because in *Union of India v. Association for Democratic Reforms (ADR)* (`(2002) 5 SCC 294`) and *PUCL v. Union of India* (`(2003) 4 SCC 399`), the Supreme Court held that a voter's right to cast an informed ballot is an integral part of **Freedom of Speech and Expression under `[📜 Article 19(1)(a)](node:art_19)`**, and in *PUCL v. Union of India (NOTA Case)* (`(2013) 10 SCC 1`), the Court directed the inclusion of the **"None of the Above" (NOTA)** button on EVMs to protect the secrecy of a voter's decision not to vote for any listed candidate!

### 10. Common Misconceptions & Their Corrections
* **Misconception:** *"If an Independent MP and a Nominated MP both join a political party 8 months after being sworn in, only the Independent MP is disqualified under the Tenth Schedule."*
  * **Correction (`CONST_TEXT`):** Under `Paragraph 2(2)` and `Paragraph 2(3)` of the **`[📜 Tenth Schedule](node:sched_10)`** (PDF p. 377), **BOTH** are disqualified! An **Independent** member is disqualified if they join a political party *at any time* after their election (`Para 2(2)`), whereas a **Nominated** member has a **6-month window** from the date they take their seat to join a political party—and if they join after 6 months (`8 months > 6 months`), they too are disqualified under `Para 2(3)`!

### 13. Knowledge-Check Assessments with Explained Answers
* **Question 7.1.1:** If a question arises whether a Member of Parliament has become subject to any of the disqualifications mentioned in **`[📜 Article 102(1)](node:art_102)`** (such as holding an office of profit or statutory disqualification under the RPA 1951, **not** Tenth Schedule defection), who decides the question and how?
  * **A.** The Speaker of the Lok Sabha acting in his sole discretion.
  * **B.** **The President of India under `[📜 Article 103](node:art_103)`, who shall obtain the opinion of the Election Commission of India and shall act according to such opinion.** *(Correct)*
  * **C.** The Prime Minister.
  * **D.** The Chief Justice of India directly at first instance.
  * **Explained Rationale:** Under `Article 103(1)–(2)` (PDF p. 75; mirrored for States in `Article 192` with the Governor + ECI), all non-defection disqualifications under `Article 102(1)` are decided by the President strictly in accordance with the binding opinion of the `[🏛️ Election Commission of India](node:inst_eci)`, whereas defection disqualifications under `Article 102(2)` / Tenth Schedule are decided by the Speaker/Chairman.
