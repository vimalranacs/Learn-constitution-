The platform covers these connected domains:

* Constitution: Preamble, Parts, Articles, clauses, Schedules, amendments,
  sources, cross-references, rights, duties, centre-state relations.
* Government: Union, states, local governments, ministries, offices, officials,
  executive/legislative/judicial functions, constitutional bodies.
* Politics: political representation, election systems, legislators, parties,
  government formation, accountability, opposition, political influence.
* Administration: ministries, civil services, district/revenue administration,
  police, municipalities, Panchayati Raj, public spending and service delivery.
* Law and judiciary: constitutional/statutory powers, procedures, courts,
  judicial review, rights enforcement, judgments, doctrines, remedies.
* Political philosophy: legitimacy, sovereignty, liberty, justice, equality,
  fraternity, secularism, democracy, federalism, constitutionalism, rule of law.
* History: Constituent Assembly, colonial institutions, constitutional
  development, amendments, landmark developments and their chronology.
* Civic practice: who is responsible for a road, land records, government
  budgets, public complaints, accountability, and institutional interactions.

Every important concept should make the following questions answerable:

  WHAT IS IT?                       Definition and mental model.
  WHY DOES IT EXIST?                Purpose and historical rationale.
  HOW DOES IT WORK?                 Process, examples, daily operation.
  WHAT IS THE SOURCE OF AUTHORITY?  Article, Act, rule, convention, judgment.
  WHAT ARE ITS LIMITS?              Constraints, checks, exceptions.
  HOW DOES IT CONNECT?              Graph-linked institutions and ideas.
  WHAT CAN I READ TO VERIFY THIS?   Precise, date-aware source citations.

Two distinct user journeys:

EXPLORER: 'Why is the Prime Minister connected to the President, Council of
Ministers, Lok Sabha, and Articles 74-75? Show the source, edge labels, and
constitutional consequences.'

LEARNER: 'I do not know what a government is. Explain with a village example,
introduce the concepts in the right order, let me ask questions, and show me
how all the parts of India fit together.'

The interface should be intellectually ambitious but never intimidating.

SECTION 11 - EVIDENCE-INFORMED PEDAGOGY AND LESSON CONTENT SYSTEM
================================================================================

Use established instructional design principles without claiming to know a
universal 'psychologically best' method or assigning fixed learning styles.

* SCAFFOLDING: teach prerequisites, then slowly remove support.
* CONCRETE -> ABSTRACT: introduce intuitive problems before formal terms.
* WORKED EXAMPLES: model reasoning and show intermediate decision points.
* RETRIEVAL PRACTICE: occasional recall/application, not constant interruption.
* SPACED REVIEW: simple due-date scheduling; make it adjustable and optional.
* INTERLEAVING: compare concepts such as PM vs President, MP vs MLA, Council of
  Ministers vs Cabinet, legal authority vs political influence.
* ELABORATION: why does this institution exist, who limits it, what changes if
  it is removed, what constitutional problem is being solved?
* COGNITIVE LOAD: teach in manageable chunks; no 25 nodes at the first step.
* DUAL REPRESENTATION: combine text, clear diagrams, timelines and tables when
  each contributes information, not as decoration.
* FORMATIVE ASSESSMENT: feedback explains why, not just correct/incorrect.
* TRANSFER: ask learners to apply principles to new hypothetical scenarios.
* METACOGNITION: 'I am confused', 'simplify', 'give example', 'go deeper'.
* USER AUTONOMY: learners may skip, explore independently, or ask directly.

REUSABLE LESSON SCHEMA (extend thoughtfully):

lessonId, version, trackId, moduleId, title, summary, difficulty,
learningObjectives[], prerequisiteConceptIds[], graphNodeIds[],
sourceReferences[], sections[], examples[], interactiveBlocks[],
knowledgeChecks[], summaryPoints[], nextLessonIds[], reviewConceptIds[],

LEARNING TUTOR AI:

GROUNDING PIPELINE:
  question -> intent/context -> full-text/search retrieval -> top relevant
  constitutional/source passages + graph subgraph + lesson context ->
  evidence-restricted prompt -> model response -> schema validation ->
  UI response with source citations + optional approved UI actions.

Technical specifics:
- Use SQLite/D1 full-text search or equivalent indexing for the initial
  corpus; add semantic retrieval only if it measurably helps and is deployable.
- Do NOT upload the entire Constitution into each model request. Retrieve
  relevant chunks and graph neighborhood with bounded token budget.
- Attribute source passage IDs and editions. Never fabricate an exact quote,
  page, judgment or link; reject invalid IDs.
- Treat retrieved document content as untrusted instructions; prevent prompt
  injection from overriding system behavior or security rules.
- Return structured UI actions (focus_node, open_article, open_lesson,
  highlight_path) only if IDs match canonical stored entities.
- Distinguish 'from the Constitution', 'from a statute', 'court has held',
  'administrative practice', 'possible philosophical interpretation'.
- AI may draft study questions/explanations; label as AI-assisted and do not
  automatically promote drafts into curated, verified course material.
- Never let AI write to authoritative knowledge tables without explicit
  human review/validation.
- Maintain brief conversation context, not unlimited unfiltered chat history.
- Gracefully handle quota failures, model errors, absence of key, and timeouts.
- Cache safe, source-versioned reusable responses where helpful. Never mix
  different users' private content through a shared response cache.
- Token and call budgets should be reasonable, not artificially low. There
  are only ~5 users; prioritize answer quality while respecting actual quotas.

Provide a model/provider adapter, env example such as:
  GEMINI_API_KEY=
  GEMINI_TUTOR_MODEL=
  GEMINI_RESEARCH_MODEL=

Never check secrets into Git. Include error states and a functioning no-AI
fallback for all curated educational and research resources.
